## How to use this

Write your answers in the space under each question. Thinking about a question and answering it in writing are different activities, and only one of them shows you where the fog is.

Start with Round 00 — get the current version out of your head before the questions start pulling on it. Then work top to bottom. Purpose constrains audience, audience constrains features, features constrain design. Answering out of order is how you end up with a beautiful screen for a product nobody needed.

**Notice which ones you skip.** That's the real output of this document.

Questions marked **[HARD]** are the uncomfortable ones — the questions with a real cost to answering honestly. Those are the ones worth sitting with.

You don't need to answer all 39. You need **Round 01**, **Round 03**, and **6.1 (gate / badge / filter)** settled before writing code, because everything downstream inherits from them. The rest can stay open and get answered by users.

**On the clock:** rethinking now is cheap; rethinking in week five is not. Give this two or three days, not two or three weeks — then commit hard and stop reopening it.

Round 06 — The identity question
6.1 Is verification a gate, a badge, or a filter?
Answer:
Verification is a filter.

Instead of hard-gating entry to the entire platform (which creates onboarding friction and legal risk), Eve allows anyone to sign up, but empowers the creator to apply gender filters to individual posts. Verification acts as the underlying trust protocol that powers those filters, letting users decide who gets entry to their specific content boundaries.

6.2 Who counts, and who decides? Write the sentence you'd publish.
Answer:
"Eve uses community vouching from verified members alongside quick automated verification to confirm your profile identity, ensuring that content set to female-only is seen exclusively by women."

*Superseded by D59 (5 Sep, see [conversation-log.md](conversation-log.md)): no automated/AI verification ships in MVP, and there's no separate "female-only" filter — the whole admitted community is women-only by admission (D30). Don't lift this sentence for a landing page or pitch deck as written.*

6.3 What happens when the system is wrong about someone?
Answer:
If a legitimate user is rejected or misflagged during sign-up, they enter a 24-hour Human Vouch Queue. They can request a quick single-tap confirmation from 2 existing verified friends on the platform, or submit a brief appeal reviewed by a human moderator within 24 hours. While pending, their account remains fully functional in public-view mode, but they cannot view gender-restricted "Women Only" feeds until cleared.

6.4 [HARD] Which failure is less acceptable to you — one man getting in, or one woman being turned away?
Answer:
One man getting into a protected space is less acceptable.

The entire premise and value proposition of Eve rests on safety and relief from unwanted male attention. If a man bypasses the system and harasses users in a protected space, trust in the platform vanishes instantly. Strictness must lean toward false positives (turning away a woman temporarily for manual review) rather than false negatives (letting an unvetted man through).

6.5 Does the product still make sense to someone who never thinks about verification at all?
Answer:
Yes. If a user ignores gender verification entirely, Eve functions as a clean, aesthetic social space with custom topic feeds and granular audience controls (e.g., posting only to close friends or specific interest circles). However, verification is what transforms it from a generic social tool into a trusted sanctuary.

Round 08 — The ones you're avoiding
8.1 What would have to be true for this to fail?
Answer:

Verification Friction Kills Growth (Highest Risk): The onboarding process is too frustrating, causing female users to drop off before completing sign-up. (Earliest signal: High drop-off rate on the verification screen during onboarding).

Bad Actors Bypass the Verification System: Men successfully create accounts to lurk on "Women Only" posts, destroying platform trust. (Earliest signal: Spike in user reports and block rates on female-only posts).

Network Effect Failure (The Cold Start Problem): Users join, see an inactive feed among their personal friends, and leave. (Earliest signal: Day-7 user retention dropping below 15%).

Incumbent Feature Copying: Instagram or TikTok launches explicit "Gender-Gated Audience Controls" for posts, neutralizing Eve's key differentiator. (Earliest signal: Announcement of demographic post-filtering in mainstream app beta updates).

High Operational & AI Moderation Costs: Scaling human review queues and real-time content safety checks consumes cash faster than the app can monetize. (Earliest signal: Monthly server and API bills outpacing user growth margins).

8.2 [HARD] What are you optimizing for: building a company, building this specific thing, or getting into a program?
Answer:
Building this specific thing. The goal is to solve a deep, real frustration experienced daily by women on major social platforms—creating a safe, high-trust digital sanctuary that actually works. Getting into an accelerator or raising venture capital are useful tools, but they are secondary to shipping a working product that users love.

8.3 If the answer in November is no, are you still building this in January?
Answer:
Yes. The problem of unwanted online harassment and performance fatigue on public social networks will still exist in January. External validation or accelerator rejections change the timeline, but they do not invalidate the user demand.
